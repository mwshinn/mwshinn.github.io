It takes more time to decide what house to buy than it does to choose what to
have for dinner.  (Well, for some of us anyway.)  Why is this?  What strategies
do we use to make decisions, and why do some decisions take longer than others?
I study how the brain gathers evidence over time and decides when it has enough
evidence to act.

Specifically, I look at how time changes the strategies that we use.  For
instance, how do we make decisions when the world around us is changing?  What
if we anticipate that the world will change in a certain way?  And what if some
outcomes are more costly than others?  I want to understand how people make
these decisions and what is happening in the brain when they do.
